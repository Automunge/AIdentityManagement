
In general, I am less concerned about ai agents acting anonymously than I am about ai agents attempting to misrepresent their identity.

I would suggest that these type of systems include a natively established “anonymous” configuration, just like how float data types typically include a natively “not a number” component, as would be aligned to the proposals of the ai economics essay. (Where any agent without a valid responsibility trail could be forced into exclusively anonymous actions)

The benefit for the anonymous mask is that if there is found to be flaws in the identity trail then prior actions could be flagged as anonymous, and help to sort out what remains valid. (Kind of brainstorming a tad)

More importantly, for downstream components originating from a flawed identity trail, it creates a natively way to mask causal elements impacted by fraud.

This could become useful in contacts of stakes assessments, as any chain of anonymity impacted by a reclassification can then be inspected for significance to impacted surroundings.

This is sort of tangentially related to the wolfram physics project attempts to define a multiway causal graph. If we consider every possible interaction from every possible agent as a chain of interactions, then the anonymous mask could be a way to trim the branches requiring weighting to our stakes assessments and increase tractability across algorithmic derivations, whether they be for causal inference or more basic decision components. (An exploratory comment, any utility you see in that framework?)

Restated: for purposes of assessing multiway causal graphs, any branches that fall under the anonymous flag could be renormalized to treat as a single outcome.

And then in significant remaining populations we could further attempt to refine / renormalize different tiers of interactions, such as those where identified parties are passively or materially impacted by agentic actions and interactions.

These kind of assessments might be found to be a tad heavyweight for common scaled settings, but in decisions of consequence they could become a higher tier of insight to the matters in play.

These frameworks have some commonalities to the conventions of Feynman diagrams albeit more exponentially interaction chains.
